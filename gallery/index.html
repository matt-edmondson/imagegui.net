The Editor by Task

Screenshots of the app, grouped by what they're for. Each one is staged and saved by ImageGui's UI test harness from the current build, so they show the editor as it is now. Pick a group to see its screenshots.

  • Opening and Starting OutImageGui opens PNG, JPEG, WebP, TIFF, GIF, BMP, and ICO images, OpenEXR renders, camera raw photos, Photoshop files with their layers, and its own .imagegui projects. A project links to the original images rather than copying them, so it stays small, and a packaged copy carries them along when the project has to travel.
  • Looking at the ImageThe canvas zooms to 3200%, turns like a sheet of paper on a desk, and carries rulers, guides, and notes. The readouts measure the finished image, and none of the viewing aids touch what gets exported.
  • AccessibilityImageGui checks an image for the people who will look at it. Text and shapes are measured against WCAG contrast at the size they’re shown, colors that look alike with a color vision deficiency are paired up, and the description a screen reader needs travels inside the exported file.
  • Tone and ColorEach layer holds a stack of adjustments that can be reordered, switched off, faded, blended, or limited to a selection at any point. Levels and curves draw the histogram of the pixels reaching them, not of the finished image, because that’s the distribution they act on.
  • Color Management and HDRAn image can work in linear sRGB or in ACEScg, the working space film, visual effects, and game studios share, where light brighter than white keeps its value. An ACEScg image is shown and exported through the ACES 2.0 output transform, so a bright sun rolls off rather than clipping, and OpenEXR files come in and go out without losing their range.
  • LayersA document is a tree of image, paint, text, fill, shape, and placed-project layers in groups. Every layer change is one undo step, an opacity drag included, and blending happens in linear light, so the gray that leaves Overlay unchanged is half the light rather than an sRGB 50% swatch.
  • Fills and Layer EffectsFill layers and effects have no pixels of their own. They’re drawn at whatever size the canvas is shown or exported, so they stay crisp, and an effect follows its layer’s shape through text edits, scaling, and clipping.
  • Texture GraphsA texture graph is a network of nodes that draws a seamless tile at any size. The same graph can fill a layer, mask one, make a selection, push pixels around, or filter the image beneath it, and every graph stays editable for the life of the project.
  • TextText is shaped with HarfBuzz and drawn from the font’s outlines, so kerning and ligatures come out as the font intends and the text stays sharp at any scale. It’s edited right on the canvas and stays editable for the life of the project, through warps, paths, and perspective.
  • Shapes and PathsShapes are vector layers with a fill and a stroke, drawn at whatever scale the canvas is. They take adjustments, masks, effects, and locks like any other layer, and combine into one outline.
  • Selections and MasksA selection is a recipe of steps rather than a channel of pixels. A rectangle, a Color Selection click, a brush stroke, or a refined edge each become a step, so a mask follows its layer when it moves, costs a few hundred bytes to save, and selects the same edge in the export as in the preview.
  • Retouching and ReshapingClone, Spot Removal, Content-Aware Fill, and Liquify keep their strokes as adjustments on the layer rather than painting into it. They follow the layer when it moves, can be switched off later, and are redrawn at full size on export.
  • GeometryCrops, turns, and resizes change where a layer sits on the canvas rather than the pixels it’s made of. Every one of them composes into a single transform per layer, so a chain of them is sampled once, through the document’s chosen filter, and four quarter turns give back exactly the image that went in.
  • ExportExport writes PNG, JPEG, WebP, TIFF, TGA, OpenEXR, PDF, SVG, and layered Photoshop files at a chosen size and quality, from the same code that draws the preview. A smaller export is evaluated at its own size rather than shrunk from a full-size one, and batches run from the app, the command line, or a node graph.
  • Game Art and Pixel ArtPixel art is drawn in whole pixels against a palette, animated with onion skin, and exported at whole multiples. Sprite sheets, trim sheets, 9-slice frames, indexed PNGs, TGA files, and normal maps come out in the forms Unity, Godot, Unreal, and the other engines read, and an export target rewrites them every time the project is saved.
  • Blender and GodotImageGui comes with an add-on for Blender and a plugin for Godot that open a texture in ImageGui and reload it every time it’s saved. Blender’s multilayer renders open as layers, and texture graphs export as full materials for either.
  • History and RecoveryEvery change can be undone, an unsaved image survives a crash, and each save keeps the one before it. Any two versions of an image can be compared down to a single level, including versions kept in git. A crash report holds no pixels or folder names and is never sent anywhere.
  • Working With GitA project and its images can live in a git repository like any other source file. ImageGui sets the repository up to show its own changes, commits a project along with everything it depends on, lists what changed between two versions down to a moved curve point, and merges two branches of a project layer by layer.
  • Commands and PreferencesEvery command is reachable by name, every shortcut can be rebound, panels dock wherever they’re wanted, and the interface scales and switches theme without a restart.
