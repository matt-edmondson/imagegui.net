The Editor by Task

Screenshots of the app, grouped by what they're for. Each one is staged and saved by ImageGui's UI test harness from the current build, so they show the editor as it is now. Pick a group to see its screenshots.

  • Opening and Starting OutImageGui opens PNG, JPEG, WebP, TIFF, GIF, BMP, and ICO pictures, Photoshop files with their layers, and its own .imagegui projects. A project links to the original pictures rather than copying them, so it stays small, and a packaged copy carries them along when the project has to travel.
  • Looking at the PictureThe canvas zooms to 3200%, turns like a sheet of paper on a desk, and carries rulers, guides, and notes. The readouts measure the finished picture, and none of the viewing aids touch what gets exported.
  • AccessibilityImageGui checks a picture for the people who will look at it. Text and shapes are measured against WCAG contrast at the size they’re shown, colors that look alike with a color vision deficiency are paired up, and the description a screen reader needs travels inside the exported file.
  • Tone and ColorEach layer holds a stack of twenty-eight adjustments that can be reordered, switched off, faded, blended, or limited to a selection at any point. Levels and curves draw the histogram of the pixels reaching them, not of the finished picture, because that’s the distribution they act on.
  • LayersA document is a tree of image, paint, text, fill, shape, and placed-project layers in groups. Every layer change is one undo step, an opacity drag included, and blending happens in linear light, so the gray that leaves Overlay unchanged is half the light rather than an sRGB 50% swatch.
  • Fills and Layer EffectsFill layers and effects have no pixels of their own. They’re drawn at whatever size the canvas is shown or exported, so they stay crisp, and an effect follows its layer’s shape through text edits, scaling, and clipping.
  • Texture GraphsA texture graph is a network of nodes that draws a seamless tile at any size. The same graph can fill a layer, mask one, make a selection, push pixels around, or filter the picture beneath it, and every graph stays editable for the life of the project.
  • TextText is shaped with HarfBuzz and drawn from the font’s outlines, so kerning and ligatures come out as the font intends and the text stays sharp at any scale. It’s edited right on the canvas and stays editable for the life of the project, through warps, paths, and perspective.
  • Shapes and PathsShapes are vector layers with a fill and a stroke, drawn at whatever scale the canvas is. They take adjustments, masks, effects, and locks like any other layer, and combine into one outline.
  • Selections and MasksA selection is a recipe of steps rather than a channel of pixels. A marquee, a wand click, a brush stroke, or a refined edge each become a step, so a mask follows its layer when it moves, costs a few hundred bytes to save, and selects the same edge in the export as in the preview.
  • Retouching and ReshapingClone Stamp, Spot Healing, Content-Aware Fill, and Liquify keep their strokes as adjustments on the layer rather than painting into it. They follow the layer when it moves, can be switched off later, and are redrawn at full size on export.
  • GeometryCrops, turns, and resizes change where a layer sits on the canvas rather than the pixels it’s made of. Every one of them composes into a single transform per layer, so a chain of them is sampled once, through the document’s chosen filter, and four quarter turns give back exactly the picture that went in.
  • ExportExport writes PNG, JPEG, WebP, TIFF, PDF, SVG, and layered Photoshop files at a chosen size and quality, from the same code that draws the preview. A smaller export is evaluated at its own size rather than shrunk from a full-size one, and batches run from the app or the command line.
  • History and RecoveryEvery change can be undone, an unsaved picture survives a crash, and each save keeps the one before it. Any two versions of a picture can be compared down to a single level, including versions kept in git. A crash report holds no pixels or folder names and is never sent anywhere.
  • Working With GitA project and its pictures can live in a git repository like any other source file. ImageGui sets the repository up to show its own changes, commits a project along with everything it depends on, lists what changed between two versions down to a moved curve point, and merges two branches of a project layer by layer.
  • Commands and PreferencesEvery command is reachable by name, every shortcut can be rebound, panels dock wherever they’re wanted, and the interface scales and switches theme without a restart.
